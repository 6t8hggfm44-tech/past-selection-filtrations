# Transfer Candidates for Universal Review

This file records PSF-derived methodological observations that may be transferable beyond the domain. They remain **provisional domain findings** until evaluated by the Universal reconciler. They are not written directly into universal truth.

## TC-2026-001 — Exact-reversal null before claiming new dynamics

- **Date:** 2026-08-10; refined 2026-08-17
- **Status:** provisional PSF-derived methodological candidate
- **Source:** P1 experimental-discriminator work; RT-2026-004 empirical-null challenge; RT-2026-005 selector-identifiability challenge; RT-2026-006 coherent inverse-amplitude mimic.
- **Observation:** A structural or interpretive framework can organize ordinary dynamics in a distinctive way without producing a distinctive physical prediction. Record formation, decoherence, apparent classicality, redundancy, and even measurement-like interactions can remain reversible in the underlying standard theory when all relevant degrees of freedom are controlled. The 2026-08-17 red-team adds a sharper warning: matching a coarse resource metric such as circuit depth or gate count does not necessarily match the nuisance mechanism. If the treatment parameter also changes the error channel, a supposedly matched control can leave a systematic residual that tracks the claimed causal predictor.
- **Candidate transferable method:** Before attributing a residual effect to proposed new dynamics, construct the strongest exact-reversal/null model available under the incumbent theory. Predeclare any new-physics parameters, vary the claimed causal predictor while matching the *mechanism-bearing nuisance exposure* rather than only coarse resource totals, and require prospective held-out residuals beyond the independently characterized null. Treat failed discrimination as scientific progress rather than rescuing the proposal by post-hoc parameter fitting.
- **Why it may transfer:** The method applies whenever a new framework shares most observables with a standard theory and differs only through an added mechanism: alternative physical models, econometric interventions, causal claims, forecasting overlays, or agent-architecture experiments can all suffer from a flexible residual term or treatment-linked nuisance process that absorbs ordinary model error.
- **Failure boundary:** Exact reversal may be physically impossible or ill-defined in some domains; in those cases the transferable lesson is to construct the strongest realizable null and explicitly account for the gap between ideal and implementable controls rather than pretending the null is exact. Matching the nuisance mechanism may itself require modeling assumptions, which must be audited rather than treated as ground truth.
- **Evidence strength:** medium. Strong within this PSF test-design problem, but cross-domain usefulness has not yet been prospectively tested.
- **Proposed Universal test:** In another domain with a claimed add-on mechanism, compare two controls that match the same coarse resource/cost while differing in exposure to the plausible nuisance pathway. Test whether mechanism-matched controls reduce false attribution or post-hoc explanation relative to coarse matching alone.

## TC-2026-002 — Quantify discriminating information before choosing the “clean” regime

- **Date:** 2026-08-24
- **Status:** provisional PSF-derived methodological candidate
- **Source:** P1 selector-echo power analysis; RT-2026-007; `experiments/ECHO_POWER_CALIBRATION_2026-08-24.md`.
- **Observation:** Two rival models may be formally different yet nearly indistinguishable in a regime that looks theoretically clean. In the PSF echo problem, matching the strongest ordinary coherent-error null to the selector law at leading order makes their observable difference begin only at higher order: log-echo separation is `O(theta^4)` and the per-shot binary-measurement KL divergence is `O(theta^6)`. Consequently, collecting more observations in the weak-interaction regime can be dramatically less informative than moving to a regime with greater curvature. But moving to stronger interventions can also create new nuisance channels, so statistical information alone is not sufficient.
- **Candidate transferable method:** Before selecting a test region because it is perturbatively simple, stable-looking, or easy to model, compute a prospective discrimination measure—KL divergence, likelihood ratio, Fisher information, expected proper-score separation, power, or another domain-appropriate analogue—between the serious rival models. Optimize the design jointly for discriminating information and nuisance/systematic control. If the rivals agree through low order, explicitly quantify the resulting sample-size or resolution penalty rather than assuming a dense local sweep will reveal the difference.
- **Why it may transfer:** Forecasting models, causal interventions, architecture ablations, and scientific experiments can all share a local or baseline regime in which competing explanations make nearly identical predictions. A test can therefore be methodologically pristine yet practically uninformative.
- **Failure boundary:** The information calculation is only as good as the rival models and nuisance assumptions. An “information-optimal” strong intervention may move the system into a new regime, change the data-generating process, or activate unmodeled confounders. The rule is therefore joint optimization, not simply maximizing formal KL or effect size.
- **Evidence strength:** medium within PSF; no independent cross-domain prospective validation yet.
- **Proposed Universal test:** On a future matched model-discrimination task, predeclare two rival models and compare a conventional low-perturbation design with a design chosen by prospective discrimination information under explicit nuisance constraints. Evaluate which produces better out-of-sample model identification without increased false attribution.
