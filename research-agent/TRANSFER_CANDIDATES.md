## TC-2026-001 — Exact-reversal null before claiming new dynamics

- **Date:** 2026-08-10
- **Status:** provisional PSF-derived methodological candidate
- **Source:** P1 experimental-discriminator work; RT-2026-004 empirical-null challenge; RT-2026-005 selector-identifiability challenge.
- **Observation:** A structural or interpretive framework can organize ordinary dynamics in a distinctive way without producing a distinctive physical prediction. Record formation, decoherence, apparent classicality, redundancy, and even measurement-like interactions can remain reversible in the underlying standard theory when all relevant degrees of freedom are controlled.
- **Candidate transferable method:** Before attributing a residual effect to proposed new dynamics, construct the strongest exact-reversal/null model available under the incumbent theory. Predeclare any new-physics parameters, vary the claimed causal predictor while matching ordinary control cost/noise exposure, and require prospective held-out residuals beyond the characterized null. Treat failed discrimination as scientific progress rather than rescuing the proposal by post-hoc parameter fitting.
- **Why it may transfer:** The method applies whenever a new framework shares most observables with a standard theory and differs only through an added mechanism: alternative physical models, econometric interventions, causal claims, forecasting overlays, or agent-architecture experiments can all suffer from a flexible residual term that absorbs ordinary model error.
- **Failure boundary:** Exact reversal may be physically impossible or ill-defined in some domains; in those cases the transferable lesson is to construct the strongest realizable null and explicitly account for the gap between ideal and implementable controls rather than pretending the null is exact.
- **Evidence strength:** medium. Strong within this PSF test-design problem, but cross-domain usefulness has not yet been prospectively tested.
- **Proposed Universal test:** In another domain with a claimed add-on mechanism, compare whether predeclared matched-control/holdout design reduces post-hoc explanation or false-positive promotion relative to ordinary retrospective analysis.
