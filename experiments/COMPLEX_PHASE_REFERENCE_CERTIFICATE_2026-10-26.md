# Complex Ramsey phase-reference certificate from a transverse conditional-action budget

**Date assessed:** 2026-10-26  
**Status:** finite-dimensional ordinary unitary quantum mechanics; a constructive experiment-calibration consequence of standard Dyson estimates. Not claimed as a novel speed-limit theorem, a hardware certification, or evidence for the optional PSF selector.  
**Manuscript baseline:** author-approved *The Accessible Quantum Past*, 9.0.2 (September 7, 2026), Sections 8, 10, 11, DOI https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22647510  
**Depends on:** `experiments/TIME_DEPENDENT_CONDITIONAL_AXIS_NULL_2026-10-12.md`; `experiments/TRANSVERSE_ACTION_PATHWISE_BOUND_2026-10-19.md`. Related P1, RT-2026-015, EXP-2026-001.

## Operational question

The earlier pathwise bound controls the **magnitude** of branch overlap in a nominal mechanism-null control. But P1's Ramsey arm is designed to infer coherent inverse-amplitude error from the **complex phase** of that overlap. Can the same calibrated transverse-action budget bound contamination of the phase-sensitive signal, without simply presuming ideal alignment?

## Statement — a second-order complex overlap bound

Let a declared closed, finite-dimensional environment with the *same pure initial state* `|ψ0>` in both system branches have piecewise-continuous self-adjoint conditional Hamiltonians

    H_±(t) = H0(t) ± B(t)/2 .

Let U0(t) solve the common Hamiltonian, and use its interaction frame:

    B_I(t)=U0(t)† B(t) U0(t),    P=|ψ0><ψ0|,    Q=I−P,
    b(t)=<ψ0|B_I(t)|ψ0>,          δ(t)=||Q B_I(t)|ψ0>||,
    φ(t)=integral_0^t b(s)ds,     J(t)=integral_0^t δ(s)ds.

For the actual branch states `|ψ_±(t)>`, define the complex overlap `χ(t)=<ψ_-(t)|ψ_+(t)>`. Then the **full complex Ramsey multiplier** obeys

    |χ(t)−exp(−i φ(t))| <= cosh(J(t))−1.                  (1)

This remains valid for noncommuting, time-dependent H0 and B, within the specified closed pure model. When `r=cosh J−1<1`, the phase of χ relative to the ideal reference is bounded by

    |wrap(arg χ + φ)| <= arcsin r,                        (2)

and `||χ|−1|<=r`. In the small-transverse-action regime `r=J²/2+O(J⁴)`: **the phase-reference error is second order in J, not first order.**

Independently, the October 19 geometric estimate gives `arccos|χ|<=J`, so `|χ|>=cos J` if `J<π/2`. The new bound does not improve every magnitude bound; it adds a phase-sensitive disk containing all modeled complex overlap values.

## Proof

Work in the common-H0 interaction frame, where the two generators are `±B_I/2`. Decompose

    D(t)=P B_I(t) P + Q B_I(t) Q,
    T(t)=P B_I(t) Q + Q B_I(t) P .

D is block diagonal and T purely P/Q-off-diagonal. For rank-one P, `||T(t)||=δ(t)`. Let the comparison propagators `V_±` be generated by `±D/2`. They preserve P and satisfy `V_±(t)|ψ0>=exp(∓iφ/2)|ψ0>`. In their respective frames `W_±=V_±†U_{I,±}`, the generators `±V_±†T V_±/2` remain block-off-diagonal and have norm `δ(t)/2`.

Set `x=J/2`. The absolute-norm time-ordered Dyson series bounds every order-n term by `x^n/n!`. Since odd orders map P into Q and even orders return it to P, defining `a_±=<ψ0|W_±|ψ0>` and `q_±=QW_±|ψ0>` yields

    |a_±−1| <= cosh x−1;       ||q_±|| <= sinh x.

The branch states decompose into a P-component `exp(∓iφ/2)a_±|ψ0>` plus a Q-component of norm at most sinh x. Consequently

    |χ−exp(−iφ)|
    <= (2(cosh x−1)+(cosh x−1)²) + sinh² x
     = 2 sinh² x = cosh J−1.

This is a standard block-off-diagonal Dyson-series bound, not a novel law of quantum records. The phase bound (2) is elementary disk geometry: a disk of radius r<1 centered at a point on the unit circle subtends at most arcsin r.

## Additional consequences

- For `J=0`, the branch multiplier is exactly `exp(−iφ)`: perfect pathwise no-record control can retain arbitrarily useful *phase* sensitivity.
- To predeclare a maximum systematic Ramsey phase bias `β<π/2`, a sufficient **within-model** condition is `cosh J_star−1 <= sin β`, where `J_star` is a simultaneous calibration-valid upper bound constructed as in the October 19 uncertainty-tube dossier.
- If independent conditional environment fragments justify an actual product overlap `χ_total=∏χ_k`, with ideal product phase `exp(−i Σφ_k)`, telescoping gives `|χ_total−exp(−iΣφ_k)|<=Σ_k[cosh(J_k)−1]`. When the sum is <1, its arcsine gives a total phase-bias bound. **Do not use this product extension for correlated fragments or shared controls.**
- This does **not** establish the selector as absent in the calibration arm if its proposed update law reacts to transient records, nor does it identify any ordinary process outside the declared family. In particular, a modelled `χ` disk must not replace Section 8's all-compatible ordinary held-out prediction set.

## Independent checks and adversarial search

1. **Exact Pauli case:** H0=0, B=b Z+d X, initial `|0_z>`. Then
   `χ=cos(λt)−i(b/λ)sin(λt)`, with `λ=sqrt(b²+d²)`, `φ=bt`, `J=|d|t`. Wolfram numerical evaluation at b=1.5, d=0.4, t=0.75 gave `|χ−exp(−iφ)|=0.0387203631278882` and `cosh J−1=0.0453385141288605`. The inequality holds; no assumption of `b=0` is needed.
2. **Piecewise conditional axes, H0=0:** Python/Numpy/Scipy exact matrix exponentials for 1,200 fixed-seed random 2-level schedules (2–11 segments, seed 20261026), integrating J and φ exactly segment-wise. No inequality failure; highest error/bound ratio `0.9966568081` (near saturation, so substantial tightening is not automatic).
3. **Noncommuting piecewise common/conditional dynamics:** 150 fixed-seed random 2-level five-segment schedules with H0 and B drawn independently from Pauli vectors. Integrated `δ(t),b(t)` via 20-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature per segment, propagated each branch by exact segment exponentials. No failure; maximal ratio `0.4588322031`. These are numerical counterexample searches, not proofs. Source for reproducibility: `experiments/code/complex_phase_reference_check_2026_10_26.py`.
4. **Sharpness check:** for b=0 and a fixed transverse axis, `χ=cos J` for `0<=J<=π`, so the actual deviation is `1−cos J=J²/2−J⁴/24+...` while (1) gives `cosh J−1=J²/2+J⁴/24+...`. Their leading coefficients coincide.

## Research disposition

A phase-sensitive calibration arm now has a **two-coordinate approximate-null certificate** in the declared closed model: the October 19 bound controls the visibility magnitude and (1) controls the complex phase-reference bias. Device implementation still requires independently acquired, context-transfer-valid Hamiltonian tubes, spectators, leakage, long-lived hidden degrees, control modes, general memory/process models, and all-compatible ordinary target prediction. This is material P1 calibration progress but **not** grounds to promote EXP-2026-001 above L3 or change N5.
